The Story

William Friedkin, In His Own Words

This compilation gathers decades of William Friedkin's interviews into one continuous listen — arranged to flow gently from the early years to the late reflections, easy to put on in the background while you work, study, or drive.

What's Inside This Compilation

William Friedkin came up through live television and documentaries before bringing that same unpolished, run-and-gun instinct to two of the defining films of the 1970s: The French Connection and The Exorcist. In between and after, there's Sorcerer, Cruising, To Live and Die in L.A., and Killer Joe — a career defined by chasing realism into uncomfortable places most directors wouldn't go.

Seven hours of Friedkin talking is seven hours of a genuinely great storyteller — blunt, funny, unsentimental about his own films (he calls some of them failures without hesitation), and full of specific, load-bearing detail: how the French Connection car chase actually got filmed, what it took to get The Exorcist's effects to work, and why he still thinks the flop Sorcerer is his best film.

Why Listen for Hours?

Because that's when it stops being an interview and becomes company. Longform Cinema Club builds every compilation for the long stretches of life — the overnight drive, the deep work session, the long run, the quiet hour before bed. Nothing jolts you. Press play and let the conversation carry you.
